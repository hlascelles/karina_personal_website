## Reasons to go:

### 1. [London](/travel/london/) and [its neighbourhoods](/travel/london-parts/), and [around London](/travel/london/#near-london).



### 2. The Lake District

* Striding Edge hike - ridge walk to/from Helvellyn. Start from Patterdale or the bus stop/car park at (54.542464, -3.058717).
* Crinkle Crags hike.
* Hike to Stickle tarn - a lake in the crator of an ancient volcano, great views and further hiking opportunities from there.
* Hardknott Roman Fort.
* Scafell Pike.
* Buttermere hike from Buttermere village, up to Red Pike, along the ridge to High Stile, and back along the north-east side of Buttermere lake. Beware of loose small rocks that make some steep parts of the route perilous, regardless if you do this hike in clockwise or anticlockwise direction.
* Rydal Cave - a short hike for a rainy day when no serious hiking is possible.

**Notes:**
* A great way to visit without a car is to take the train to Windermere, stay there and take [buses](https://lakedistrictonboard.com/transport/lake-district-buses/) to starting points of different hikes. Usually a pdf of current bus schedule can be found.



### <a name="edinburgh"></a> 3. Edinburgh and around

**Edinburgh:**
* Stockbridge: This is the best area to stay in Edinburgh. It has great food shopping (Stockbridge Market on Sundays, I.J. Mellis Cheesemongers Stockbridge, George Mewes Cheese), museums (National Galleries of Scotland - Modern One and Modern Two, Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh), walks (along Water of Leith, past beautiful Dean Village), cocktails (The Last Word) and less tourists.
* Edinburgh New Town: Seeing the city from Calton Hill is a great place to start. Both The Gardener's Cottage and The Lookout by Gardener's Cottage are great restaurants. There are many cocktail and whiskey places on my to-visit list - The Scotch Malt Whisky Society, Panda & Sons, Lucky Liquor Co, Bramble Bar, Nightcap.
* Edinburgh Old Town: Edinburgh Castle, Surgeons' Hall Museums, National Galleries of Scotland - National, National Museum of Scotland, St Giles' Cathedral. My all time favourite postcards come from Edinburgh Arts & Picture Framers on Nicolson Street. The Outsider is a great restaurant; Hey Palu is awesome for cocktails. The Meadows is where the locals enjoy the outdoors.
* Leith: Leith Farmers Market on Saturdays. A cocktail at Nauticus is a great way to start/end your exploration of Leith.
* Holyrood Park and beyond: Hiking up to the Arthur's Seat is a must, and don't miss the Scottish Parliament Building. Craigmillar Castle - I prefer this one to the touristy Edinburgh Castle. Pentland Hills Regional Park is excellent for beautiful long walks. Area around Royal Observatory Edinburgh is nice for a short walk. Holyrood Architectural Salvage is a fun place to explore.
* Cramond Island - it's fun to walk there, but check the tide schedule first.
* Still on my to-do list is a boat tour from the [Scottish Seabird Centre](https://www.seabird.org/boats) to see puffins. Once there, I'll explore Tantallon Castle too. And perhaps Glenkinchie Distillery too.

**Near Edinburgh:**
* [Whitelee Windfarm](https://www.whiteleewindfarm.co.uk/whitelee-windfarm-about-us) near Glasgow - walking among wind turbines and sheep. To reach it without a car, take a bus to Eaglesham and walk from there to Whitelee Windfarm Visitor Centre, where you can get a warm drink to set yourself up for a long walk - 215 turbines connected by 130km of trails.
* Glasgow: Riverside Museum (a magnificent building by Zaha Hadid).



### <a name="bristol"></a> 4. Bristol and around

Southville:
* [Sweven Coffee](https://www.swevencoffee.co.uk/) - my favourite coffee place in Bristol.
* Graffiti hunting - a lot of it is along North street, Luckwell road, West street and East street. See [UpFest map](https://www.upfest.co.uk/theme/Live/upfest-map.pdf) or this [Google My Maps](https://inspiringcity.com/2024/08/25/upfest-presents-2024-all-the-street-art-and-murals/). Upfest Gallery itself is on my wishlist. The street art festival happens every 2 years, so there is a steady supply of new murals.
* On my wishlist: COR Restaurant. Albatross Café might be nice.

Spike Island:
* [Root](https://www.rootbristol.co.uk/) - my favourite restaurant in Bristol. From here you also get great views of cranes outside M Shed museum and the surrounding area.
* My favourite view of Bristol's colourful houses is from Bristol Marina (51.448102, -2.610924).
* Underfall Yard is fun. Visit the Engine House (51.44685, -2.618236); engine demos run on weekend mornings. 
* A tiny ferry runs between SS Great Britain Ferry Landing and Hannover Quay. The journey is fast and cheap. And you get to catch a glimpse of SS Great Britain ship.
* On my wishlist: Spike Island art gallery (free).

Central Bristol:
* Walk up to Cabot Tower for great views. Climbing to the top of the tower (free) is on my wishlist.
* Wills Memorial Building is beautiful on the outside. I believe the inside is magnificent too, taking a [tour](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/university/visit/tower-tours/) is on my wishlist.
* On my wishlist: Bristol Cathedral, [The Mount Without](https://www.themountwithout.co.uk/) (an intriguing venue, not sure if easy to visit), Arnolfini Arts (free art gallery), tasting menu at [Wilson's Restaurant](https://www.wilsonsbristol.co.uk/). Millennium Square in the evening might be fun.
* Graffiti hunting along Stokes Croft. This street, that changes its name from Stokes Croft to Cheltenham road to Gloucester road, holds the title of UK's longest independent shopping street. If you get hungry along the way, I'd try one of the Sri Lankan restaurants - Nadu or The Coconut Tree.

Old Town:
* The area around Broad street and Corn street has a lot of beautiful old buildings. [This pdf](https://bristololdcity.co.uk/sites/default/files/old-city-heritage-trail-map.pdf) makes for a fine self-guided tour.
* Full Court Press is a nice coffee place. Urban Tandoor is a nice Indian restaurant.
* On my wishlist: tasting menu at [Adelina Yard](https://www.adelinayard.com/).

Clifton:
* Clifton Suspension Bridge. A [hard hat tour](https://cliftonbridge.org.uk/whats-happening/) is on my wishlist. There are also other tours.
* On my wishlist: exploring Clifton Village and a long walk through Leigh Woods.

**Near Bristol:**
* Just outside central Bristol: on my wishlist are Aerospace Bristol museum, University of Bristol Botanic Garden and Arnos Vale Cemetery.
* Bath: The Roman Baths. City's unique architecture, good examples include The Circus and The Royal Crescents. Bath Abbey decorated with carvings of [angels climbing the ladders](https://curiousrambler.com/the-upside-down-angels-of-bath-abbey/). Paxton & Whitfield is a great cheese shop. On my wishlist: Museum of Bath Architecture.
* Stonehenge is exciting. Avebury is on my wishlist.





### 5. <a name="birmingham"></a> Birmingham and around

**Birmingham:**
* The Jewellery Quarter - a lovely neighbourhood. Great cocktails at [40 St Pauls](https://www.40stpauls.co.uk/) gin-centered cocktail bar. Still on my to-visit list: Ikigai at 1000 Trades cocktail bar and Damascena Jewellery Quarter for brunch. I can highly recommend the area around St. Paul's Church as the best place to stay during your visit.
* Digbeth (including the Custard Factory) - the neighbourhood full of street art. I used this [map](https://visitbirmingham.com/things-to-see-and-do/graffiti-art-of-digbeth-walk-p1346341) as a rough guide, but keep exploring and you'll find more, for example [The Brolly Works public parking](https://goo.gl/maps/bd1hN4YEQARatiHc9). Still on my to-visit list: Chance & Counters board game cafe.
* Gas Street Basin - an area of canals. Ikon Gallery is great.
* City Centre - I loved the vegan tasting menu at Land, Indian food at Ark Restaurant and cocktails at Fox and Chance. Still on my to-visit list: The Pineapple Club Birmingham cocktail bar.
* South part of Birmingham: Lapworth Museum of Geology - a paradise of rocks and fossils. Fluorescent rocks on the upper floor are magnificent! Chakana - an amazing Peruvian/Japanese restaurant with numerous vegan options. Moseley Bog - the park that inspired landscapes in The Lord of the Rings, nice for a short walk. Still on my to-visit list: cocktail bars Couch Stirchley and Bar Ikigai.

**Near Birmingham:**
* Coventry Transport Museum - an excellent museum. [Thrust SuperSonic Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ThrustSSC) is on display.
* Lichfield cathedral is beautiful. The rooftop tour is still on my to-do list.
* Warwick Castle is on my wishlist.
* My favourite place in Stratford upon Avon is MOR Bakery.
* My favourite places in Leamington Spa are Five Rivers (Indian food) and Spa Town Coffee.



## Notes:
* Long distance hike on my wishlist: [King Charles III England Coast Path](https://www.nationaltrail.co.uk/en_GB/trails/england-coast-path/) and [Wales Coast Path](https://www.walescoastpath.gov.uk/plan-your-visit/?lang=en) for hikes with finest coastal landscapes.
* Trainline app is the best place to see all UK trains. The app is free to use to track trains in real time, but you pay commision if you buy tickets through the app. It makes my life so much easier, that I'm ok with paying the commision.
