### Reasons to go:

<a name="cairo"></a>
### 1. Cairo

1.1 Maadi neighbourhood:

* Best place to stay for work-from-homing. Almost everything is west of the metro line, you'll be constantly crossing over the track if staying east of it.
* Barista - great coffee.
* Maharaja Indian restaurant Maadi Rd 9 - great Indian restaurant. Their other branch nearby has the same menu, but I prefer this one.
* Syria Market (29.962726, 31.278228) - a nice deli and cafe.
* Gourmet Egypt - a nice supermarket.
* Ratios Bakery - amazing bakery, and you can eat them in Fyngan Coffee Shop in the same building.
* Zooba - Egyptian restaurant with many vegan options.
* Villa Sumatra Coffee Degla - great coffee and breakfast options.
* Street 154 in Maadi is a lively traditional mmarket with vegetables, etc.
* The Lebanese Bakery - nice place for flatbreads, many vegetarian options.
* One of the magnificent buildings (29.96009, 31.272027).

Heliopolis:
* ???

Central Cairo:
* Cairo train station (Ramsis station) is beautiful inside, find an excuse to visit even if not taking any trains.


Zamalek island:
* Cairo Tower - on Friday morning there was no queue and I enjoyed my visit, but I don't think it is worth it if you spend a long time queueing. Due to air pollution the views are severily limited. Views from the outdoor observation deck (standard ticket) are better than from the restaurant with limited-transparency glass.

Giza: 
* Sound and light - didn't add much.
* Worst place to stay - there is nothing but hotels and souvenir shops, no chance for a walk without souvenirs being literally stuffed into your face and it is hard to get an Uber to get out of there. Come see the pyramids and the museum and get out.


Notes:
* Landing at Cairo airport is smooth - you can buy a local sim card at 24/7 kiosks in the airport (card payments accepted) and use it to call an uber.
* Metro card - buy at any metro station that has a working self-service machine. Some machines might be top-up only (not issuing new cards), some accept only coins, most give no change. But it is worth it to persevere and obtain a metro card to use metro conveniently. You can choose the intial top-up value that will be stored on the card based on the exact cash you have. If you are buying single paper tickets from the ticket office, exact cash is appreciated. Single ticket prices are shown in Google Maps directions, the price varies depending on the journey length.
* At Cairo train station (Ramsis station) you can buy foreigner train tickets. They are no longer hand-written as in 2023. Information center for foreigners near the station entrance is useless. Foreigner ticket office on the messanine floor (near the food court) is where you pay for the tickets (USD cash or card payment for extra fee). You can buy tickets for any train route here, even the ones that don't start/end in Cairo, and you can buy many days in advance. They can only sell you tickets for trains on which tourists are allowed. I don't think you can tell from https://egytrains.com/trains/ which trains you'll be allowed to take, it depends on police presence on the train, not train type.





### 2. Port Said

* Watching ships pass through Suez Canal. Viewing platform at (31.260468, 32.311234) is probably the best place to be. You can see a tentative ship schedule [here](https://www.myshiptracking.com/estimate?pid=3167), get a more direct evidence from opening/closing times of El Nasr floating bridge via [this app](https://itsallwidgets.com/el-nasr-bridge) (if you have an iphone), or find another source of information. I was in Port Said for a short time and didn't see any ships.
* Beautiful buildings in the coastal area - Al Salam Masjid (from outside), The Lighthouse of Port Said (the old one), Villa Fernande (31.266757, 32.310087), a sequence of beautiful churches (31.267261, 32.308839), (31.267134, 32.307853) and (31.267303, 32.307453), etc.
* Ferial Garden is nice, Metro Market is a well-stocked shop on the corner of it. El Mesala Garden and the Fish market are still on my wishlist.
* Taby’s bakery is increadible - savoury pastries, sweet stuff and coffee are all fantastic. No toilet.
* This falafel restaurant (31.271442, 32.296015) is on my wishlist.
<nbsp></nbsp>
* Riding the free ferry across the Suez Canal. They depart from around here (31.259443, 32.310683) every couple of minutes, it basically functions like a bridge. I enjoyed riding these ferries there and back many times, and there were local families doing these joyrides too.
* Port Fuad. St. Mark Coptic Orthodox Church (my passport was checked on the way in) - the ceiling features paintings of dinasours, a flamingo and a flying lion head. Salt Mountain (31.233573, 32.3198) - for a small fee (5 EGP when I visited) you can run up and down big piles of salt and watch local couples and families taking selfies.
<nbsp></nbsp>
* A taxi ride from Port Said to Alexandria is long but exciting. You'll see new purpose built towns and infinite palm groves, fish farms around (31.35739, 32.048828) and boat builders around (31.578085, 30.98231). I believe you'll see more driving towards Alexandria then in the other direction, where there will be lines of traffic between you and the coast. I booked my taxi from Egypt Taxi Transfer info@egypttaxitransfer.com and had a great experience.
* Taking a daytime train from Cairo to Port Said is high on my wishlist, as it follows Suez Canal closely between Ismailia and Port Said. Ideally on the day when many ships will be passing by. I took an evening train and could not see anything through the window.















**Notes:**
* Careem taxi app worked well in Port Said. On the other side in Port Fuad I had no luck with apps, but numerous local taxis were happy to take me anywhere for a flat fee of 15 EGP. 
* The new Suez Canal Museum in Ismailia is trying to be modern and engaging - touch screens (only in Arabic), holograms, a moving model of [Ever Given incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Suez_Canal_obstruction). You are supposed to see different rooms in a particular order, so you are constantly sheparded by staff who are perplexed by your inability to understand Arabic. The whole experience is not really enjoyable and the content of the museum is not very technical. With current restriction on which trains the tourists are allowed to take, it is not possible to visit Ismailia for just a couple of hours, so I'd say it is skipable. If you do end up here, falafel sandwiches at this local favourite (30.590022, 32.26975) are great.

### 3. Alexandria

* Alexandria National Museum. Three floors each covering one of the periods - Pharaonic, Graeco-Roman and Coptic-Islamic. Start from the basement - Pharonic period. kaffine_65 is a great coffee place nearby.
* Alexandria Bibliotheca. The 3 main things to see are the building itself from the outside, the huge main library hall and the museums downstairs. Ticket office around (31.207902, 29.908687), combined tickets provides access to the Antiquities Museum and the Manuscripts Museum.
* Ancient Roman Theater in Alexandria. Entrance at (31.194089, 29.903641).
* Qaitbay Citadel. Entrance at (31.213142, 29.885673). Employees begging for tips made it less enjoyable than other places in Alexandria.
* The two beautiful mosques - Sidi Morsi Abu al-Abbas Mosque and Sidi Yakout Al-arsh Mosque. Without taking my shoes off (weary of the scams related to that), I admired the buildings from the outside (the best bit) and looked inside through the entrance.
* Church of the Great Martyr George Shatby is beautiful from the outside. I believe the Greek Orthodox Cemetery just outside it is worth a visit, but I didn't have time to figure out how to get access to it.


* Walk along the bay (26 July street) - beautiful views and many beautiful buildings along the way (Egyptian Chamber of Commerce, Alexandria Naval Unknown Soldier Memorial, etc.). It is also magical in the evening. 
* Exploring historic parts of Alexandria (inland from the bay) - many beautiful buildings include Bank Misr Building (31.19662, 29.900337), Mohammed Feiter Building (31.198487, 29.900739), etc.






* Moustafa GAD Restaurant Central Bank Branch - great Egyptian food, loads of vegan options. It is open from early until late, so I've been there for breakfast, lunch and dinner. Menu only in Arabic, prepare your Google Translate in camera mode. Staff speaks enough English, I never had a problem ordering in English over my many visits. As you approach, it looks like fast food, but open the door and you'll be transported into a nice sit down restaurant. There are other GAD restaurant branches.
* Mohamed Ahmed Restaurant - great Egyptian food, the menu is mostly vegan. Menu in English. For me this was a good starting point to familiarise myself with typical dishes, before graduating to GAD Restaurants with menu only in Arabic.
* Délices Patisserie Alexandria - great cofee and many breakfast options.













**Notes:**
* Uber works well in Alexandria.
* Staying near Saint Catherine Cathedral was super convenient - most places I wanted to visit were reachable on foot or via a short Uber ride if in a rush.








### Planning your trip:
* I found [Ryan's](https://batnomad.com/category/must-see-egypt/) and [Nicki's](https://nickipoststravelstuff.com/africa/egypt-travel/) blogs useful. [This post](https://www.reddit.com/r/travel/comments/yrldtt/our_independent_trip_to_egypt_was_perfectly_fine/) and [this one](https://medium.com/@ivyxvine/egypt-travel-without-a-tour-how-to-get-around-egypt-on-a-budget-1a8cba981c56) reassured me that it is a good idea to travel in Egypt like in any other country, being on an all-inclusive multi-day trip is not the only or even the best option. I've also read a lot of horror posts about Egypt, which turned out to be very far from reality. If you've never travelled outside Europe/North America, you might find Egypt a bit difficult to navigate. If you've travelled in India, you'll find Egypt pleasantly easy. I believe the reason most blogs about India say it is magical and many posts about Egypt say it is horrible, is that people going to India roughly know what they are signing up for while many people going to Egypt have only seen photos of the hotels and the pyramids, and expect it to be not much different from Europe.

* https://egymonuments.com/ - Buy tickets for most things online. (good for opening hours and ticket buying). Good precaution for scams too. I strongly recommend that you click on the “return to portal” link on the purchase confirmation page. This will take you to a further page with a “download your tickets” link which you should use as it avoids any delays in the email tickets arriving. I've read that - As a general rule, tickets for sites which can be bought online are card only. Tickets for sites not available online are still cash only.
* https://www.soundandlight.show/en
* https://www.toursbylocals.com/
* https://egytrains.com/trains/
* https://southsinaitravelegypt.com/tours/ - create tours for the returning travelers, the ones who seek more than historical sites, and those who’d like to walk off the-beaten-path. 
* Flying or sleeper train https://abelatrains.com/#/.
* Many things closed on Friday.
* Email info@egypttaxitransfer.com to book a long-distance taxi. You agree on price over email, pay the driver USD cash on arrival.

* If you are flying in and you can get 30 day visa on arrival, it is the best option. It is really more of a simple sticker, than a visa. [Applying online](https://www.visa2egypt.gov.eg) in advance seems to have only disadvantages. I've not found a way to enter Egypt with the right to remain for more than 30 consecutive days, but I was told there are ways to extend once you are inside.
