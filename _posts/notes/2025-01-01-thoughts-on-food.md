Good ideas come when you are dicing an onion. 
Hundreds of onions later, I decided to lay out all my thoughts about food on a single page. 

If I ask myself - what do I want from food? - three things come to mind.
Most importantly, food should contain the nutrients my body needs. 
It's better if it is low in things that are detrimental to my health. 
It's even better if it makes me happy.  
Let's unpack each of these goals.

### Goal #1 - eat food that contains things my body needs

Roughly speaking, there are macros, things that human bodies need in large quantities, and there is a long list of micros, things that are needed in small quantities.
There are four macros - protein, fibre, carbs and fats.
Food packaging implies that we should aspire to eat more protein and fibre, and less carbs and fats.

##### Protein

Unlike carbs and fats, human bodies cannot store protein.
This has two implications.
First, it is important to eat enough protein every day, we've all heard that. 
Second, which gets far less attention, is that excess protein, since it cannot be stored in the body, is stripped of nitrogen to be used (or stored) as carbs. 
The liver and the kidneys deal with excess protein, which means that extreme quantities of protein could be harmful for them. 

I have two questions - how much protein do I need? and how much protein do I eat?
To answer the latter, I used [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/), and a chicken breast as a benchmark. 
One 200g chicken breast contains 45g of protein; for comparison I, as a vegetarian, might eat 200g of chickpeas (18g), tofu (34g) or paneer (44g).
I usually eat two big meals a day, which according to [my calculations](/notes/protein-calculations/) is equivalent to 1.5 chicken breasts (±0.5) per day.

The amount of protein a human body needs seems to be on the order of magnitude of 1g per kilogram of body weight. 
I could not find any sources that recommend less than 0.5g or more than 2g per kilogram of body weight. 
Our individual protein requirements depend on factors like age, genetics, level of physical activity, recent injuries, etc.
From an evolutionary perspective, human bodies have evolved to deal with shortages but not with excess, so my intuition is that less is better than too much. 
Since my craving for protein, i.e. how much I eat on a day when I am not counting, is on the order of magnitude of what my body might need according to scientific evidence, I won't be supplementing with a protein powder.
